About

I care about what happens after the model is trained.

I started with quantum algorithms — Shor's, Grover's, phase estimation — and kept following the same instinct: take the system apart until the numbers mean something. That instinct moved me toward machine learning engineering, where the hard part is rarely the model. It is the data contract, the evaluation split, the latency budget, the alert that nobody trusts.

The network detection platform is the clearest example. It scores Zeek connection logs with a gradient-boosted classifier and streams results to a dashboard — but the part I am proudest of is the restraint: measured p50 and p95 latency, replay data tagged so it cannot masquerade as live traffic, and a published cross-dataset probe that scored zero recall. A detector that hides its failure modes is a liability.

How I work

  • 01

    Measured, not asserted

    Latency, false-positive rate and throughput are reported because they were measured.

  • 02

    Failures stay visible

    A zero-day probe that scores 0.00 recall is published next to the 0.973 F1.

  • 03

    Reproducible by default

    DVC stages, pinned dependencies, seeded splits and locked pipelines.

  • 04

    Promotion is a decision

    Quality gates compare a candidate against the baseline before anything ships.
